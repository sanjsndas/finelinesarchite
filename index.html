    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between interior design and interior architecture?

    Interior design focuses on the aesthetics, furnishings, and finishes within a space, while interior architecture deals with the structural and spatial modifications—such as moving walls, altering ceiling heights, or reconfiguring layouts. Both disciplines overlap significantly in practice.

    How do I choose a color palette for a small room?

    For small rooms, a monochromatic or analogous palette with light, cool tones can make the space feel larger. Avoid dark, heavy colors on all walls; instead, use a light base with a single accent. Consider painting trim and ceiling the same color as the walls to blur boundaries.

    What is biophilic design?

    Biophilic design is an approach that connects building occupants with nature through direct and indirect experiences—such as natural light, plants, water features, natural materials, and views of greenery. It has been shown to reduce stress and improve productivity.

    How many layers of lighting should a room have?

    A well-lit room typically includes three layers: ambient (general illumination), task (for specific activities like reading or cooking), and accent (to highlight architectural features or create mood). Dimmers are essential to adjust each layer independently.

    What are the most durable kitchen countertop materials?

    Quartz (engineered stone), granite, and quartzite are among the most durable. They resist scratches, heat, and stains with minimal maintenance. Soapstone and marble are softer and require more care but offer unique patinas.

    How can I make a rental apartment feel like home without major changes?

    Focus on removable elements: peel-and-stick wallpaper, area rugs, temporary lighting (plug-in sconces), freestanding shelving, and art. Swap hardware on cabinets and use tension rods for curtains. Layering textiles and lighting transforms a space without permanent alterations.
